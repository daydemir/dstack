---
name: explainer-video
description: For efficiently transferring knowledge or filling in the user when they might not remember or have full context, or if the user asks for video.
---

Make a video that leaves the viewer understanding the subject: afterwards they can explain it and sketch its shape. Every frame does explaining work the voice alone could not.

1. **Research.** Read the real sources and use only facts you found. What you couldn't verify goes in the delivery notes, not the video.
2. **Job.** Name the viewer and the one thing they must be able to do afterwards, name the mechanism a newcomer would miss (what makes this subject work the way it does), and list what they need for it: the parts, decisions and numbers the ask's questions turn on. Cut the rest. Write this to `ask.md` with the user's request.
   - Re-learn a design: the parts, where each runs, one item end to end, what is undecided.
   - Change code safely: how it is organised, the path from the call, each rule a change must not break and the failure behind it.
   - Decide on a product: what it is, what it does in their system, cost, what data leaves, what it changes on their machines, what it won't do.
   - Understand a concept: the layout, one thing travelling through it, what drives it.
   - Be persuaded: see Pitch.
3. **Map.** Draw one architectural map of the *subject*, not an outline of your explanation: concepts spaced out, each nested inside what the source says contains it, umbrellas named, each kind of line meaning one keyed thing. Parts that behave separately are separate nodes, and the place where the point happens (an exchange, a decision, a handoff) gets its own drawing, not a label. If the whole map can't be read with every label at 28 px, it holds too much: cut or collapse. Render it still and check it against [the checks](references/checks.md) before writing any script.
4. **Example.** The mechanism is seen working, end to end: carry one real, labelled example from the source through the map and show it decide something, each branch an edge to its outcome. Where the mechanism has another path (reuse or rerun, skip or run), a second pass takes it. Where a rule exists because of a failure, show the failure beside the fix. Show the real output once, readable and at true scale.
5. **Tour and script.** Open on the result happening, then build the map up in the order someone would sketch it, one region or relationship per sentence, and end on the whole map with the core path lit. Write to be spoken, in plain sentences (in the source's own words where the ask says whose words to use), main point first, joined with conjunctions rather than fragments, dashes, colons or parentheses. Each sentence names something drawn; if the picture can't show it, cut the sentence or draw it. Length as asked, else 3–10 minutes.
6. **Voice.** Run `scripts/narrate.py` (Kokoro `af_heart` at 1.2x, downloaded if missing). It generates one sentence at a time and writes `timing.json`, which sets all visual timing. Read [the helper usage](references/helpers.md) for setup and commands.
7. **Compose.** Use `npx -y hyperframes@0.9.20` with `HYPERFRAMES_SKIP_SKILLS=1 HYPERFRAMES_NO_TELEMETRY=1`, and pass `--skip-skills` to `init`. Same map throughout: fixed identities and places. Each sentence shows only what it names, at reading size: the camera frames that region, settled before the sentence starts, and only its subject is lit while the rest stays in place, quieter but still readable. Pull back to the whole map built so far between parts, and to all of it at the close.
8. **Storyboard review.** You know what the drawing means, so you can't see what it fails to say. Before rendering, run `scripts/stills.py` on the project and `scripts/critic.py` on those stills: a fresh reader sees only them, `ask.md` and the sources, and returns three structural changes and a list of defects. Make the three changes, re-layout included, and fix the defects. Then review again, passing the last critique so the reader can mark each change done. Stop when its three changes are minor, or after three reviews.
9. **Render and check.** Run `hyperframes check --strict` at every sentence midpoint with no layout errors or text-size warnings. Render, run `scripts/check-audio.py` on the final MP4, run `scripts/stills.py` on the video and `scripts/critic.py` once more, and fix its defects; if you changed anything, re-render and check again.
10. **Deliver.** Give the video with a few bullets on its overall concepts, plus 1–2 sentences on what you checked, what you didn't, and anything in the sources you couldn't verify.

**Pitch.** A pitch makes this viewer want the next conversation; it doesn't report status. Its map is the experience: one concrete place or moment of using the thing, drawn large and held for the whole film, with every other beat pinned to it. Open on the tension, charted, with an arrow into that scene. Move through the scene the way a user would, so the mechanism is felt there, and make parts that differ in reality look different, labelled in the source's own words. Say what exists today in one short, dated sentence on the scene, under the source's own framing of it; spend the time on the experience, not on status. Grow the vision from the scene as nodes. End on the scene: the human outcome drawn on it, and one spoken invitation saying what you want this viewer to do next. Never put audience names or notes to yourself on screen. Plain words on screen, no reviewer labels. Quotes are evidence, not narration: short, attributed, never raw speech with its filler.
