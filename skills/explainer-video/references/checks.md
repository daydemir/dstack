# Checks

One list, used twice: by the maker on the still map before scripting, and by the critic on the finished stills.

## Map
1. Every box inside a box is something the source says, and a region means one thing everywhere (where it runs, who owns it). A rule or note sits on the thing it governs. Each concept appears once.
2. Each line style means one thing, and the key is fixed on screen from its first use, every glyph keyed before it appears. One glyph per kind of object for the whole film.
3. Lines land on the node they mean, carry arrowheads, never cross unbridged or run through a label; glyphs point the way the flow goes.
4. Only true guesses are dashed and marked, in plain words. Undecided alternatives are drawn ghosted and keyed as options. What follows directly from the source is stated and drawn solid.
5. Everything lives on the map. Cost, status, setup, decisions and outcomes are pinned to the node they are about, never in a caption bar, footer, side list or card that replaces the map. Detail opens as a close-up anchored to its owner, with the rest dimmed but present.

## States
6. A drawn state is true at that moment. Nothing flows through something drawn closed.
7. One state per card. Alternatives shown together are labelled alternatives.
8. Nothing appears before the voice reaches it. Highlights release when the sentence ends.
9. Example-only states are cleared, or kept exactly as the example left them, on the closing map.

## Teaching
10. Each sentence's subject is drawn, lit and readable at a glance in its frame, and only it is lit. Nothing is taught only by caption, list or voice.
11. The mechanism that makes this subject what it is happens on screen, end to end, through one real example. Where it has another path (reuse or rerun, skip or run, pass or fail), a second pass takes it.
12. Every branch is an edge to its drawn outcome (retry loop, let through, blocked), not a list of cases. A process that repeats is drawn as one cycle of its states.
13. Real names, real output and real scale where the source has them: the actual comment, message or screen, readable; the true size of a motion beside any enlargement. Things that differ in the subject look different on screen; no "A" and "B" where the source names them.
14. One numbering scheme, and a step number can't be read as a count. Figures match the voice and keep the source's qualifiers.
15. Quotes verbatim, never spliced. Jargon defined once or cut.

## Frame
16. Text 28 px or more, inside its box, uncovered, not cut by the frame edge.
17. The opening shows the result happening, then the map builds from the top level. The last frame is the whole map answering the ask, the core path lit and the rest present, quieter but every label still readable, with the takeaway or invitation on it, up for at least the last three seconds of narration. No title card replaces it.
18. A close-up stays locatable.
