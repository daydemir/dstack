# Formatting messages

Order the message by what the reader must do first, and spend emphasis only where the eye should land. When restructuring an existing message, keep every fact, number, option, ask and hedge, and reword only where the structure needs it. Sentence style belongs to unslop and long documents to human-readable. This skill covers the shape of one message.

## Order

1. **Clock first.** Whatever has a deadline or blocks the reader opens the message: the deadline, then the ask. Bold the time and nothing else in that sentence (`Legal signs off by **Monday noon ET**`). A `---` rule separates it from the rest when the topics are separate.
2. **The thing, then the case.** Show the proposal (the copy, the answer, the artifact) before any reasons for it.
3. **Caveats beside what they change.** A caveat or optional change sits directly under the item it modifies, labeled for what it is (`**Optional tweak:**` for a change the reader may make, `**Heads-up:**` for a catch), and it names its target ("All" in the headline) so the reader needn't scan back.
4. **Reasons last, building to the payoff.** Group reasons of the same kind together and end on the one that ties to the reader's goal.

## Emphasis

- Bold carries the skeleton: the clock time alone (`**2:59am ET**`), the proposal line, section labels and list lead-ins. Read only the bold and you should get the message in priority order.
- The proposal is the one fully bold line. Numbers and evidence around it stay plain so they support it.
- Section labels share one weight and form a left-margin spine. Each label names everything in the block under it (`**Headline and stats I'd use:**` when the quote holds both).
- Bold lead-ins within one list share one grammatical shape.

## Asks

- Inputs the reader must supply go in a bulleted list, one per line, so they can reply against it. Keep the source's urgency words ("soon") and conditions ("if you want in").
- An ask and a recommendation stay as separate as the source has them. Link them only when the source does.

## Markdown

- Put a blank `>` line between a quoted headline and its supporting bullets.
- Use one bullet marker (`-`) throughout, including inside quotes.
- Keep the source's wording for times and amounts ("in about 2½ hours" when the source said "in about two and a half hours").

## Check

Done means both pass:

- **Fidelity.** Every fact, number, option, ask and pointer from the source is present, and nothing is new. Hedges keep their strength: "I think" and "mainly" survive into the new wording ("I think the warehouse is the best fit, mainly because…").
- **Skeleton.** The bold alone reads as the message in priority order.
